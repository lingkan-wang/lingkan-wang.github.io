## Overview

<Statement>Guild managers ran rosters worth millions on gut feel — because the data that could save a fading streamer was scattered, stale, and buried.</Statement>

Kuaishou (Kwai) is a leading short-video and livestreaming platform with over 600 million monthly active users. Its livestreaming business runs on **guilds** — agencies that recruit streamers, coach them, and earn a commission on what their streamers make. **Voyager** is Kuaishou's internal platform for those agencies and creators.

I designed a **weekly reporting and churn-alert dashboard** inside Voyager for guild managers on PC. It reorganizes the metrics that matter, surfaces at-risk streamers from revenue trends *before* they churn, and turns a reactive, gut-feel routine into a proactive one.

## User research

To make the tool scale across guilds — and hold up for overseas operations — I combined qualitative and quantitative methods: interviews and surveys, analysis of behavioral and platform data, and a steady back-channel with the operations team who talk to guild managers every day.

### What is a guild?

A guild is a management group that recruits and supports streamers. Day to day, guilds:

- Provide coaching, technical support, and content guidance
- Help streamers grow their audiences and revenue
- Earn a commission on the performance of the streamers they manage

### What managers told us

I interviewed **six guild managers** across top, mid, and emerging tiers. Three themes came through clearly.

## Solution

Each strategy became a view in the dashboard. Here is what shipped.

### Guild data overview

I synced the four headline metrics — Total Revenue, Total Live Hours, Active Streamers, and Total Streamers — and gave each one Day-over-Day, Week-over-Week, and Month-over-Month deltas, so a manager reads momentum at a glance instead of reconstructing it.

<Figure src="/work/kwai-guild-dashboard/solution-01.png" alt="Guild data overview: four synced KPI cards — total revenue, live hours, active streamers, total streamers — each with day-, week-, and month-over-month comparisons" zoom caption="The overview — four core metrics, each carrying day-, week-, and month-over-month deltas." />

Each card pairs the current value with last month's, then splits momentum into three deltas — day-, week-, and month-over-month — color-coded green for up and red for down. A manager reads both *direction* and *speed* at a glance, with no exports or spreadsheets in between.

### Tier classification

The platform rates each guild on its streamers' performance and sorts streamers into three income tiers — $50+, $600+, and $800+ monthly — so managers always know where a guild stands and who to invest in.

<Figure src="/work/kwai-guild-dashboard/solution-02.png" alt="Tier classification: the current guild level, with streamers grouped into three monthly-income tiers" zoom caption="Tier classification — guild level up top, streamers grouped into $50+, $600+, and $800+ tiers." />

The guild's current level (A) sits up top. Below it, the count of streamers clearing each monthly-income threshold, each carrying its own day-, week-, and month-over-month trend — so a manager sees not just *how many* streamers earn well, but whether the income distribution is climbing or slipping.

### Top this month

A ranked Top 10 — the highest week-over-week growth among streamers clearing 10M diamonds — with gift contributions quantified, so wins are easy to spot and reward.

<Figure src="/work/kwai-guild-dashboard/solution-03.png" alt="Top streamers this month, ranked by week-over-week growth with gift contributions quantified" zoom caption="Top this month — ranked by WoW growth, with each streamer's gift contribution." />

Each row shows total diamonds received and total live hours next to the growth figure, so a manager can separate a streamer who's genuinely accelerating from one simply putting in more hours. It makes wins easy to *spot, reward, and reverse-engineer* — what is the top of the roster doing that the rest could copy?

### Churn alert

The piece that changes the workflow: the Top 10 at-risk streamers by income drop, with Effective Broadcast Days alongside, so managers can step in while it still matters.

<Figure src="/work/kwai-guild-dashboard/solution-04.png" alt="Churn alert: top at-risk streamers ranked by income drop, paired with their effective broadcast days" zoom caption="Churn alert — at-risk streamers by income drop, paired with effective broadcast days." />

It's the mirror image of the top-performer view, and the one managers open first. Streamers are ranked by how steeply their earnings are falling, with effective broadcast days beside the trend so a manager can tell a quiet week apart from a real exit. Surfacing decline *before* a streamer goes dark turns the dashboard from a report into a prompt to act.

## Reflection

This project was a turning point in how I think about designing for decisions. Unlike a consumer interface, this dashboard served guild managers who needed both **clarity and control** to act on fast-changing performance data — and it had to earn their trust every time they opened it.

The biggest takeaway was learning to balance information density with usability. I had to stop asking "how do I visualize this data?" and start asking **"what decision should this view enable?"** — and that shift reshaped the information architecture and which metrics to lead with.

Through interviews and iteration I also came to appreciate the role of predictive design. The churn alert was more than a feature — it moved the product from passive reporting to **proactive intervention**, and pushed me past the UI into system thinking: how data flows, what should trigger action, and how to put the right signal in front of the right person at the right time.

Overview

KuaiShou's live-streaming business runs on guilds — agencies that recruit streamers, coach them, and take a cut of what they earn. But the managers who run those guilds were flying blind: the data they needed to catch a fading streamer or back a rising one was scattered, stale, and buried.

I designed a weekly reporting and churn-alert dashboard for guild managers on PC. It reorganizes the metrics that matter, surfaces at-risk streamers from revenue trends before they churn, and turns a reactive, gut-feel routine into a proactive one.

Understanding the guild world

To make the tool scale across guilds — and hold up for overseas operations — I ran a multi-layered research process: interviews and surveys, analysis of behavioral and platform data, and a steady back-channel with the operations team who talk to guild managers every day.

A guild is a management group that recruits and supports streamers. Day to day, guilds:

  • Provide coaching, technical support, and content guidance
  • Help streamers grow their audiences and revenue
  • Earn a commission on the performance of the streamers they manage

I interviewed six guild managers across top, mid, and emerging tiers. Three themes came through clearly:

01

Flying blind

Managers leaned on intuition and self-reported updates, because timely data simply wasn't there.

02

No way to compare

There was no like-for-like way to compare streamers within a guild.

03

A pull toward alerts

Strong, repeated demand for structured alerts and one clear, centralized view.

From insight to opportunity

Guild managers need timely, structured, actionable insight — to monitor performance, catch churn early, scale their operations, and lift the quality of every stream.

Mapping the pain points against that need surfaced four opportunities to design against:

  • Slow to spot churn → surface key streamer metrics earlier, and make risk alerts something you can act on.
  • Scattered tooling → pull fragmented tools into one centralized, structured dashboard.
  • Unclear tiers → visualize guild-tier progression and make strategic growth legible.
  • Information overload → restructure the information architecture so the page leads with what matters.

Design strategies

01

Guild overview metrics

Daily, weekly, and monthly trends, visualized clearly so managers spot growth or decline early and act on it.

02

Tier classification

Transparency and tier-progression cues, so managers can prioritize higher-value streamers and time promotions.

03

Churn-risk alerts

A predictive alert built on activity drops and income decline, so at-risk streamers get attention before they leave.

04

Top-performer highlights

Surface outperformers by income and growth, benchmark them against guild averages, and replicate what works.

Solution

Four moves carried the redesign — each one closing a specific gap from the research.

Guild data overview

I synced the four headline metrics — Total Revenue, Total Live Hours, Active Streamers, and Total Streamers — and gave each one Day-over-Day, Week-over-Week, and Month-over-Month deltas, so a manager reads momentum at a glance instead of reconstructing it.

Tier classification

The platform rates each guild on its streamers' performance and sorts streamers into three income tiers, so managers always know where a guild stands and who to invest in.

Top this month

A ranked Top 10 — the highest week-over-week growth among streamers clearing 10M diamonds — with gift contributions quantified, so wins are easy to spot and reward.

Churn alert

The piece that changes the workflow: the Top 10 at-risk streamers by income drop, with Effective Broadcast Days alongside, so managers can step in while it still matters.

Reflection

This project reshaped how I think about designing for decisions. A dashboard for guild managers isn't a consumer screen — it has to give people both clarity and control over fast-moving data, and earn trust every time they open it.

The hardest, most useful lesson was balancing density with usability. I stopped asking "how do I visualize this data?" and started asking "what decision should this view enable?" — and that question drove the information architecture and which metrics to lead with.

The churn alert is the part I'm proudest of: it moved the product from passive reporting to proactive intervention. It pushed me past the UI into system thinking — how data flows, what should trigger action, and how to put the right signal in front of the right person at the right moment.

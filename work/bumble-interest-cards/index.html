Bumble BFF & Bizz · 0→1 concept

Interest Cards — turning everyday plans into low-pressure ways to meet.

Most of Bumble isn’t on Premium, and its location features are easy to miss. Over five weeks I designed a location-based discovery layer that lets people broadcast what they’re up to — and a Premium moment that feels earned, not nagged.

The problem

Bumble has a huge, active community — but in BFF and Bizz, the people most worth meeting are often the ones who are literally nearby: the runner at the same gym, the founder at the same coworking space. Three things got in the way.

01

Premium is invisible

Most users never see why Premium is worth it — the value shows up too late, as a wall rather than a benefit.

02

No reason to reach out

A cold profile gives you nothing to say. People hesitate to make the first move without a shared, in-the-moment context.

03

Location feels risky

Sharing location is a hard yes to ask for — especially for women — unless the personal upside is obvious and stays in their control.

The insight

I interviewed three people who’d recently relocated and scanned how dating, social, and events apps handle discovery. Three findings shaped the direction.

01

Context beats profiles

People respond easily when a shared activity gives them something concrete to talk about — far more than to a profile alone.

02

Safety is the gate

Several said they’d happily share location, but only when the benefit was clear and they controlled exactly what they revealed.

03

Intent is seasonal

Friends and professional connections convert most in spring and summer, when people are most open to meeting in person.

Competitive scan

The concept

Interest Cards are short, location-anchored posts — “Looking for a running buddy at Tepper Gym this week.” You publish what you’re up to; people nearby discover it and join. The whole thing slots into Bumble’s existing structure, so there’s almost nothing new to learn.

Designing for conversion

Rather than gate the whole feature, I put Premium where it removes real friction — seeing who you’re about to meet, and reaching past your immediate block. Each upgrade trades on a clear, felt benefit, so the moment converts without feeling coercive.

Takeaways

01

Safety and monetization are the same design

The controls that earn a location opt-in — choosing what you reveal, to whom — are the very things that make Premium worth paying for. Designing one well designs the other.

02

Context is the cheapest growth lever

An activity-anchored prompt did more to lower the barrier to a first message than any profile change could. Give people something to react to, not just someone.

03

Earn the upgrade, don’t wall it

Placing Premium at the peak of curiosity — the blurred photo of someone you already want to meet — converts without feeling coercive.

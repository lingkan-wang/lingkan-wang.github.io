Brief

Under rising customer complaints and growing expectations for smart-home automation, I was tasked with optimizing the DEEBOT X2 autonomy experience at ECOVACS Robotics. The X2 already had advanced mapping and obstacle recognition, but users hit persistent friction: complex manual map editing, confusing parameters, and failures avoiding high-risk obstacles like pet waste. I focused on bridging the gap between technical capability and perceived intelligence — an AI hosting mode built on floor-material recognition, simpler mapping, and better high-risk detection — to cut cognitive load and build trust in autonomy.

Research

To find where autonomy breaks down in real use, we combined customer-complaint analysis, App Store review mining, and interviews with pet owners and first-time users.

What we heard

Problem statement

Although DEEBOT X2 is positioned as fully autonomous, users still hit friction in setup, decision-making, and high-risk scenarios. The system functions — but it doesn’t consistently feel effortless or trustworthy.

01

Autonomy still feels manual

Users frequently edit maps after generation. Setup introduces hesitation and erodes confidence in the system’s intelligence from the very start.

02

Too many decisions for the user

Cleaning modes and suction levels require manual selection, but users lack clarity on how to choose — adding cognitive load and weakening the “one-click” promise.

03

High-risk failures break trust

In pet households, failing to avoid pet waste is catastrophic. Even occasional errors sharply damage trust in autonomous cleaning.

The mission

How might we make autonomy feel effortless and reliable — by reducing manual intervention, simplifying decisions, and strengthening trust in high-risk moments?

Opportunities

01

Make mapping feel truly automatic

Auto-mapping existed, but users still split rooms, renamed spaces, and fixed boundaries. Reduce those corrections so mapping feels confident on its own — especially for first-timers who expect plug-and-play, not supervision.

02

Reduce decision fatigue in setup

Instead of asking users to configure the robot, shift the decision to the system — letting AI read floor types and usage patterns to recommend or auto-apply the right settings.

03

Design for trust in high-risk moments

Pet-waste failures damaged trust. Prioritize high-risk object recognition and clear system feedback so the robot feels safe and dependable, not unpredictable.

Solution 01

AI Smart Hosting

A one-tap intelligent cleaning mode. Instead of configuring suction, water flow, or room selection, the system reads spatial data, floor types, obstacles, and past patterns in real time to decide the optimal strategy itself.

AI hosting as the default

The AI toggle sits at the whole-home cleaning entry — visible and accessible, not buried in settings. One tap applies intelligent defaults across every room.

Flexible control for advanced users

Advanced parameters move to a separate custom tab to keep the primary flow clean; users can still draw targeted zones on the map when they want to intervene.

Making AI visible and trustworthy

A first-time explainer sets expectations, a live status shows AI is driving the clean, and post-clean summaries — route, dirt map, metrics — make decisions traceable.

Solution 02

AI Auto Mapping

An intelligent spatial-recognition system that scans, understands, and generates a structured floor plan on the first run — detecting walls, room boundaries, and layout in real time, with no manual setup.

Guided first-time mapping

A contextual pop-up gives simple prep tips (clear clutter, keep doors open); a clear entry point guides users into the flow when no map exists yet.

Transparent pause & stop

Mapping runs long, so interruptions are handled explicitly: early-exit warnings prevent accidental data loss, and pause states say whether progress will continue.

Post-mapping refinement

Users name the floor for clarity in multi-floor homes, and can adjust room labels and layout for minor corrections.

Solution 03

Pet Mode

An experimental Lab feature for homes with pets. It prioritizes risk avoidance — enhancing high-risk object detection and adjusting cleaning behavior to prevent contamination.

Clear conflict feedback

Pet Mode and Granular Dirt Mode optimize for opposite goals — safety vs. particle pickup — so a mutual-exclusion toggle and a clear dialog explain the trade-off instead of silently overriding the user.

Product overview

Designing the app meant designing to the hardware. I worked from the X2’s real form factor — its brushes, mopping pads, and sensor array — so on-screen behavior matched what the robot could actually sense and do.

Takeaways

01

Start with real household behavior

People don’t think in cleaning modes — they think in outcomes: “make my home clean, and don’t bother me.” The value came from removing decisions, not adding options.

02

Design for trust, not just automation

Even capable AI won’t be trusted unless it feels safe. Pet-waste avoidance was an emotional safety requirement; clear feedback turned “is it safe?” into “I can walk away.”

03

AI should feel invisible but dependable

The best moments were when users didn’t notice the system thinking — only that the tasks disappeared. Great home automation is quiet confidence: “it just happens.”
